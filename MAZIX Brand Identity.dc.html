01
STRATEGY
We don't sell AI. We build intelligent systems, and we are accountable for how they run.
The category problem
Every AI company looks like an AI company. Gradients, glow, sparkle. The visual noise makes serious engineering indistinguishable from a demo.
The position
Mazix is an engineering firm. AI is the material, not the promise. The identity borrows from architecture and industrial design, not from software marketing.
The test
Remove the word "AI" from every surface. If the brand still reads as world-class software, it is correct. If it collapses, it was hype.

04
TYPOGRAPHY
Geist
A neo-grotesque drawn for engineering interfaces: closed apertures, flat terminals, a near-mechanical rhythm, and a monospace sibling cut on the same skeleton. It carries a poster headline at 300 and a 12px table label at 500 without ever changing personality.
Why not the alternatives
Söhne and Suisse International are the right register but licensed per-seat, which slows a company that ships in public. Inter is free and excellent but now reads as default. Neue Haas Grotesk is beautiful and too editorial for dense UI. Geist gives the Swiss register, the mono pair, and no licence friction.

DIRECTIONS
Five directions, built on the same 48-unit construction grid. Each is shown flat black, reversed, cobalt, and at 16px.
DIRECTION 01
Module
Modular intelligence
MAZIX
Five identical squares on a 3×3 grid. No square is special; the X exists only in their relationship — intelligence as an emergent property of ordered parts. It is the most animatable of the five and the most honest about what the company does.
DIRECTION 02
Keystone
Abstract system
MAZIX
Two solids narrowing to a shared waist — a load path, a compression joint. It means nothing literally, which is the point: it can only ever come to mean Mazix. Strongest at large scale and in engraving.
DIRECTION 03
Plan
Maze, architecturally
MAZIX
Two walls of a floor plan, drawn at constant stroke — a route through complexity rather than a puzzle. Reads as architecture, not gaming. Risk: meander forms are common in heritage marks, so it is the least ownable.
DIRECTION 04
Splay
M / X monogram
MAZIX
An M whose outer stems splay outward, so the counters open like an X. One letter, two readings, no monogram cliché. Warmest of the five — but a letterform mark ties the brand to the name forever.
DIRECTION 05
Allocation
Grid & optimisation
MAZIX
One square, subdivided unevenly — resources allocated, capacity assigned. Quietly clever, but at 16px it becomes a generic four-panel and loses its argument. Recommended as a pattern module rather than the mark.

Direction 01 — Module
It survives at 16px, embroiders, engraves and laser-cuts without modification, assembles itself in motion, and generates the whole graphic language as a by-product. It is the only direction where the logo and the design system are the same idea.

08
WORDMARK
MAZIX
GEIST MEDIUM · −0.045em · FOUR DRAWN CORRECTIONS
M — flattened apex
The middle vertex stops short of the baseline. It stops the M reading as two V's and quietens the letter, which sets the tone for the whole word.
A — flat crossbar, no apex serif
The apex is cut flat and parallel to the baseline, echoing the M. The crossbar sits low so the counter matches the Z's aperture.
X — asymmetric weight
The descending diagonal is 4% heavier than the ascending one, the classical correction. It also makes the X the optical anchor at the end of the word.
Spacing — I opened, ZI tightened
Diagonal pairs (A-Z, I-X) trap light, so those gaps close; the straight-sided Z-I pair opens. The result reads evenly at 11px and at three metres.
