EXPLORATION
100 MARKS
One hundred marks, ten families, one construction grid. Ten survive.
Every mark is drawn inside the same 48-unit square, in flat #0E0E0E, using only 90° and 45° geometry. No mark relies on colour, gradient, transparency or stroke variation to work. Families A–J are deliberately pushed past the comfortable answer: the first row is the obvious territory, and the sheet gets stranger as it descends.

Cut immediately (41 marks). Anything requiring a tonal step to be read — J2, J3, J5, J6, J7, J9, J10, F4, E7, H5 — fails monochrome and fails 16px in one stroke.
Cut for prior art (23). D2/D3 read as Greek key, G7/G9 as a generic frame, H1/H2 as pinwheel stock marks, I5 as a crosshair, B4 as a media control.
Cut for weak meaning (16). E3, E4, E6, E9, I9 are charts. A chart is a symptom of the work, not the company. B5, F8, C10 carry no argument at all.
Cut for fragility (10). B1, B8, C4, D7, D10, G10, I1, I4, I7, I10 all close up, clog or vanish below 24px. A logo that needs a size floor is not a logo.
SHORTLIST
TEN
Ten marks that survive monochrome, 16px and a fifteen-year horizon.
RANK 01
G1
Counter
INV / 24 / 16
CORE CONCEPT
The X is never drawn. A solid plate is cut, and the intelligence is what was removed. Engineering as subtraction — the discipline of taking away until only the necessary remains.
GRID & GEOMETRY
40×40 plate on the 48 grid, 4-unit margin. Two 7-unit channels at exactly 45°, inset 9 units so they stop short of the corners — the plate stays one continuous piece.
HIDDEN MEANING
Four solid quadrants held together by their own corners: a system whose parts only stand because of how they meet. Also, unavoidably, an X — but one you find rather than one you are shown.
STRENGTHS
Enormous optical mass — it holds a page at 16px and a wall at three metres. Perfectly self-inverting. Literally a laser-cut file. Nothing else in enterprise software looks like it.
WEAKNESSES
Heavy. It dominates any lockup, so the wordmark must sit at 0.55× the plate height. On dark UI it needs the inverted cut, which is a second asset to govern.
SCALABILITY
Best in class. The channel is 15% of the width, so it survives embroidery, hot foil, sandblasting and a 16px favicon without a single modification.
MOTION
The cut travels: two channels wipe across the plate at 440ms, one 60ms behind the other. Or the four quadrants separate a half-unit and return. Precise, never bouncy.
FAVICON & WORDMARK
The strongest 16px silhouette of all hundred — a filled square is the most legible shape a tab can hold. Pairs with Geist Medium at −0.045em, cap height matched to the channel width.
RANK 02
A1
Module
INV / 24 / 16
CORE CONCEPT
Five identical squares. No part is special; the X exists only in the relationship between them. Intelligence as an emergent property of ordered, unremarkable components.
GRID & GEOMETRY
3×3 of 13-unit modules with 1.5-unit gutters on the 48 grid. Every module is identical to the tenth of a unit; the centre sits on the exact optical middle, not the mathematical one.
HIDDEN MEANING
Four agents and one orchestrator. Read the other way, four corners of a plan and a central column. It is also the quincunx — the oldest mark for a stable arrangement of five things.
STRENGTHS
The only shortlisted mark that generates an entire graphic language for free: the module tiles into patterns, grids, dividers and slide furniture. Logo and design system are one idea.
WEAKNESSES
Squares-on-a-grid is the most crowded space in tech identity. Distinctiveness comes from the discipline of the execution, not the idea — which is a harder thing to defend.
SCALABILITY
Excellent to 16px, where the gutters hold at exactly 0.5px. Below that the diagonal fuses — acceptable, since it still reads as an X.
MOTION
Best of the ten. Modules travel in along grid axes, upper pair then lower, 60ms apart, settling with no overshoot. The mark literally assembles itself.
FAVICON & WORDMARK
Clean at 16px but lighter in mass than Counter, so it recedes in a busy tab strip. Sits naturally beside Geist Medium; module height equals cap height.
RANK 03
F1
Keystone
INV / 24 / 16
CORE CONCEPT
A load path. Two solids narrow to a shared waist and pass force through it. Nothing literal — which is precisely the ambition: a shape that can only ever come to mean Mazix.
GRID & GEOMETRY
Two trapezoids, 38 units wide at the outer edge tapering to 8 at the waist, 4-unit separation on the horizon line. 180° rotational symmetry, exact.
HIDDEN MEANING
A compression joint, an hourglass, and — at a glance — the waist of an X. It also reads as two systems narrowing to a single interface, which is what an integration actually is.
STRENGTHS
The most abstract and therefore the most ownable. Beautiful at large scale, extraordinary in engraved metal, and utterly unlike any current AI company.
WEAKNESSES
Meaning must be paid for with time and media spend — abstraction has no shortcut. And at very small sizes the 4-unit gap wants to close, flattening it into a solid bowtie.
SCALABILITY
Good, with a 16px variant required: the waist gap opens to 6 units below 20px. That is a governance cost, not a fatal one.
MOTION
The two halves slide together along the vertical and stop one unit apart — a mechanical seating. Slower and heavier than Module; more ceremonial.
FAVICON & WORDMARK
Solid at 16px once the gap is opened. Its diagonal edges echo the A and X of the wordmark, giving the tightest formal rhyme of any pairing here.
RANK 04
C1
Splay
INV / 24 / 16
CORE CONCEPT
An M whose outer stems splay outward so the counters open into an X. One letterform, two readings, no monogram cliché.
GRID & GEOMETRY
Stems at 15° from vertical, stroke 9 units, mitred at every join. The centre vertex stops at 27 of 48 — two-thirds down — so the apex never touches the baseline.
HIDDEN MEANING
The M and the X of the name occupy the same letter. Structurally it is also a portal frame — two splayed columns carrying a load.
STRENGTHS
Instantly attributable — a letterform mark never has to earn its meaning. Warmest of the ten, and the easiest for a client to describe over the phone.
WEAKNESSES
It ties the company to its current name for ever, and letter marks are the most contested territory in software. It also cannot generate a graphic language.
SCALABILITY
Good to about 20px. Below that the inner V fills and it reads as a solid triangle — a 16px variant with a widened counter is mandatory.
MOTION
Limited. The stems can splay from vertical into position, which is elegant once and tiring twice. Letterforms rarely animate without looking like a title sequence.
FAVICON & WORDMARK
Adequate at 16px. Pairing is its weak point: an M beside the word MAZIX stutters, so the lockup must use the symbol alone or the wordmark alone, never a tight pair.
RANK 05
B3
Converge
INV / 24 / 16
CORE CONCEPT
Four wedges point inward from the edges and stop. The subject of the mark is the void they define — a decision arrived at from four directions.
GRID & GEOMETRY
Four congruent isoceles triangles, 22-unit base on each edge, apex 5 units short of centre. 90° rotational symmetry, no overlaps.
HIDDEN MEANING
The negative space is a rotated square — the module from A1, arrived at from the outside. Four inputs, one unresolved centre: the honest shape of a system that keeps a human in the loop.
STRENGTHS
Genuinely original and quietly tense. Reads as an aperture, a compass rose and a joint detail at once, without illustrating any of them.
WEAKNESSES
Four separate shapes is one more than the eye wants to resolve in a second. Camera-aperture associations are close by, and that is a well-used metaphor.
SCALABILITY
Fair. The apexes thin below 24px and the wedges lose their points; a 16px variant with blunted tips is required.
MOTION
Excellent — the four wedges close toward the centre and stop short. It is the most narratively obvious animation of the ten, which is also its risk.
FAVICON & WORDMARK
Middling at 16px — the void closes and it greys out. Pairs well with a wide-tracked wordmark, badly with a tight one.
RANK 06
D1
Plan
INV / 24 / 16
CORE CONCEPT
Two walls of a floor plan. The maze in the name, drawn as architecture rather than a puzzle — a route through complexity, already solved.
GRID & GEOMETRY
Constant 7-unit stroke, butt caps, all turns at 90°. Two open-ended paths, offset by one stroke width so the negative channel between them is exactly 7 units too.
HIDDEN MEANING
The gaps are doorways. Read as a diagram it is also a state machine: an entry, a branch, and a terminal — the anatomy of every agent Mazix ships.
STRENGTHS
The most literally architectural mark on the sheet, and the only one that says "maze" without a single gaming cue. Ages very well.
WEAKNESSES
Meander geometry is heavily occupied — heritage institutions, museums, Greek-key marks. Least ownable of the ten and the hardest to clear at trademark.
SCALABILITY
Fair. At 16px the 7-unit channels close to under a pixel and the two walls merge into a blob. Needs a simplified single-wall variant below 24px.
MOTION
Strong: the paths draw themselves, stroke-dash style, as if being set out on site. Risk of feeling like a loading spinner if it ever loops.
FAVICON & WORDMARK
Weakest favicon of the shortlist. Pairs beautifully with a wide-tracked wordmark at signage scale, where its detail finally pays.
RANK 07
E1
Allocation
INV / 24 / 16
CORE CONCEPT
One square, subdivided unevenly. Capacity assigned; resources allocated. The picture of an optimisation, not of a machine.
GRID & GEOMETRY
40×40 field cut at 21/15 horizontally and 21/15 vertically with a 4-unit gutter — deliberately not the golden section, so the eye reads it as decided rather than derived.
HIDDEN MEANING
A tatami plan, a treemap, and a plot subdivision. Four unequal parts that still form a perfect square: efficiency without symmetry.
STRENGTHS
Calm, dense and premium. Excellent as a container — it crops, tiles and becomes a slide layout more naturally than anything but Module.
WEAKNESSES
At a glance it is four rectangles, and it never resolves into a second reading. No X, no M, no hook — memorable only after repeated exposure.
SCALABILITY
Very good — solid masses, generous gutters, no thin geometry anywhere. It simply becomes a slightly divided square at 16px.
MOTION
Good: the four panels resize to new proportions and settle, a live allocation. Elegant, though it edges close to looking like a dashboard widget.
FAVICON & WORDMARK
Legible but anonymous at 16px. Pairs cleanly with the wordmark at any weight — it asks nothing and gives little.
RANK 08
H3
Aperture
INV / 24 / 16
CORE CONCEPT
Four identical blades in rotational symmetry — an X that never touches itself. The centre is a square of held-open space.
GRID & GEOMETRY
One 6×15 bar, instanced at 45°, 135°, 225° and 315° about the centre. A single primitive, four transforms — the whole mark is one number and one rotation.
HIDDEN MEANING
Four processes running in parallel around a shared, deliberately empty core. Focus, without drawing an eye or a lens.
STRENGTHS
Precise, balanced and very cheap to construct — the kind of mark that looks inevitable. Beautiful in embroidery, where the four blades stitch identically.
WEAKNESSES
Rotational blade marks are a recognisable current fashion, and fashions date. Also the closest of the ten to reading as a loading indicator.
SCALABILITY
Good. The 6-unit blade holds at 16px, though the central square closes to roughly a pixel and the mark starts to read as a solid X.
MOTION
Dangerously good — it wants to spin, and spinning is exactly what this brand must never do. Constrain it to a 12° settle, or do not animate it.
FAVICON & WORDMARK
Strong, distinctive favicon. Pairs best with a lightly tracked wordmark; its 45° blades echo the X terminals well.
RANK 09
F5
Chamfer
INV / 24 / 16
CORE CONCEPT
A square with one corner chamfered — the machinist's keying mark that tells you which way the part goes in. Orientation, tolerance, fit.
GRID & GEOMETRY
40×40 plate, one 14-unit chamfer at exactly 45° on the top-right. That single cut is the entire mark — the most economical drawing on the sheet.
HIDDEN MEANING
Also a document with a folded corner, and a chip package key. It says "manufactured to a specification" in one gesture, which is the whole positioning.
STRENGTHS
Maximum mass, minimum drawing. Unmistakable at any size, in any process, and it makes a superb container for cropped photography and slide corners.
WEAKNESSES
Almost too simple to protect: a chamfered square is a shape, not a mark, and it belongs to no one. Carries nothing of M, X or the name.
SCALABILITY
Flawless — a solid mass with one straight cut. Nothing to lose at 16px, nothing to gain at 16 metres.
MOTION
Poor. A single solid can only slide or scale, and the chamfer gives it one gesture — the corner cutting away — which is a one-time trick.
FAVICON & WORDMARK
Perfect favicon mass, ambiguous identity. Best used as a plate that the wordmark sits inside rather than beside.
RANK 10
B9
Crossing
INV / 24 / 16
CORE CONCEPT
An X interrupted at the crossing. Four approaches, and a junction left deliberately unbuilt — the point where the system defers to a person.
GRID & GEOMETRY
Four 9-unit strokes on the 45° diagonals, each stopping 10 units from centre with butt caps. The void is a perfect square rotated 45°.
HIDDEN MEANING
The eye completes the X that isn't there — the viewer does the last piece of work. A quiet argument about how good systems leave room for judgement.
STRENGTHS
Confident and unusually restrained. Four identical strokes make it trivial to reproduce in any medium, and the negative centre is genuinely memorable.
WEAKNESSES
Sits close to a close/dismiss icon, which is a bad neighbour for a company logo. It is also the least architectural of the shortlist.
SCALABILITY
Fair. Below 20px the four strokes fuse into a solid X and the concept — the gap — disappears entirely.
MOTION
Good: the four strokes extend toward the centre and stop, the near-miss doing all the work. Restrained and quietly tense.
FAVICON & WORDMARK
Weak favicon — reads as a close button in a tab strip. Pairs cleanly with the wordmark at header sizes and above.

RECOMMENDATION
Counter
A solid plate with an X cut out of it. The mark Mazix should own.
It argues the positioning
Intelligence Engineering is a subtractive discipline: you remove until only the necessary structure is left. Counter is that idea as a shape. Nothing was added to make an X — material was taken away, precisely, and the X is what remained.
It is the opposite of AI branding
AI identity is additive: glow, gradient, particles, sparkle — light emitted into space. Counter is a heavy, opaque, cut object. It looks manufactured rather than rendered, which is the entire distinction Mazix is selling.
It wins where it matters
A filled square is the most legible object a 16px tab can hold, and Mazix will be seen in a browser tab and a dashboard corner a thousand times for every billboard. It is also self-inverting: one geometry, both modes, no variants.

The runner-up, and when to take it
Module (02) is the safer, more generative choice: it builds the entire graphic language and animates better than anything else here. If Mazix expects to launch a product suite with many sub-brands, take Module. If Mazix wants one unforgettable mark, take Counter.
